# Interview script

25 minutes. The first 20 are their workflow. The last few are the hypothesis, and only if the workflow produced a real incident. You may stop at minute 20 and never mention Vognary. That is a valid interview.

Do not show the website, the price, or a diagram until section C. Do not ask them to upload a bill. If they offer a file, refuse it and ask them to describe a range instead. Say why: the public site is not cleared for customer financial data.

Open: "I want to understand the last time the company agreed to spend more on a model, an API, or cloud. I am not here to sell. I may describe an idea at the end if it is relevant. You can stop whenever you want."

## A. The last real commitment — minutes 0 to 15

Ask, then wait. Do not fill silence with an example from your own company.

1. Think of the last time someone agreed a new or larger commitment for a model API, an AI subscription, a GPU block, or a cloud account. What was it, roughly when, and why was it needed?
2. Who was allowed to say yes? Where was that yes written down — chat, email, a ticket, a card limit, nothing?
3. Was there a number that was not to be crossed? Who set it?
4. Which product, if any, enforced that number? Did it stop usage or a charge, or did it only send an alert or a report after the fact? Name the screen if they remember it (provider console, card, sheet, Zoho, something else).
5. What did the later invoice or usage page show? Did it match the yes? If they give a figure, write the range they volunteered. Do not push for an exact rupee or dollar amount.
6. If something was higher than expected, what did you do the next day? Who resolved it, and how long did that take?
7. How often does a version of this happen — this quarter, this year, or almost never?
8. What did it cost when it went wrong, in money, time, or a delayed decision? "Nothing much" is an acceptable answer. Record it.

## B. What they already use — minutes 15 to 20

9. Walk through the tools that were open: provider dashboard, company card, accounting, a sheet, WhatsApp. What is each one for?
10. What have you already tried that you stopped using?
11. Is the current way good enough? What would make you change it?
12. Is there a commitment in the next 30 days that does not have a ceiling yet? If they say no, do not argue.
13. Who else has to agree before the company pays for a new tool? Is that the same person who approves the model bill?

If they have not described a concrete incident, skip section C. Thank them. The ledger row will say the hypothesis was not tested with them.

## C. The hypothesis — only after A and B

Say this, and no more:

"One idea is a record, not a block. Before a material AI, API, or cloud commitment, a named person writes the purpose and the most it may cost. When the invoice arrives, the record shows whether the bill stayed inside that number. It would not decline the card and it would not stop the provider. The provider or the card would still be what actually stops spend, if anything does."

Then:

14. What is missing from that, given what you just described?
15. What would you distrust?
16. What would make it unnecessary — a sheet, the provider's own hard limit, a card limit, an accountant?
17. Only if they have described a real cost or a repeated mess: what would one month of that record be worth? Let them name a number. If they ask you, you may say the current public pilot is ₹14,999 once, for one policy, up to ten proposals, and four short reviews, and that this price is unproven. Then ask whether that is plausible, high, or irrelevant. Do not bargain.
18. If, and only if, they recognise the problem and are willing to keep talking: explain the boundary in one sentence. "We cannot take a real bill on the public site until a security review is finished. A later conversation would use redacted or synthetic numbers unless that review is done." Ask whether they would take that later conversation. A yes to "send a deck" does not count. A yes is a date or a named next step.

## Do not say

- That Vognary stops overspend, saves a percentage, or prevents fraud.
- That competitors cannot do this.
- That their company has a problem they have not described.
- A trillion-dollar story, a market size, or a promise of enforcement later.
