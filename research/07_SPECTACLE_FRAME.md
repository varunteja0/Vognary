## 2. What REAL monopolies-in-waiting in finance have (the five mechanics)

1. **A colossal flow** is attached ($2.6T payments revenue pool / $2.1 quadrillion flow; cross-border $190T) — sitting money, not discretionary subscriptions.
2. **A compounding per-decision asset** every use adds to (audit records / depth of data).
3. **Neutrality** — they judge rather than transact; a judge cannot be competed against by the parties it judges.
4. **Self-distributing seat** — embedded at rails/banks/brokers so the buyer's own operations carry it (TradingView embedded its chart engine into hundreds of brokers; revenue found them).
5. **A standard** others must reference to be credible.

The trading domain fails 1 by construction — its entire retail software revenue is measured in tens of millions of dollars annually (verified in §1). Journals fail 1 & 5; replay tools fail 1; prop-B2B risk engines partially fail 1. **The only candidate class satisfying all five is the machine-money rails/standards seat** (the locked P1+P2 track).

## 3. Decision (as of today)

1. **The "spectacular product" is not a name anyone can honestly coin today — it is the machine emerging from Gate-3.** What is decided by evidence, not by desire: (a) primary track = locked Stage-1 authorization-proof seam (Tier-4 wave, Nov 1 G1); (b) if it passes G2, the 90-day build is the founder's first at-bat at the only product class matching the demanded impact; (c) any dream product handed down today would be invented day-0 data — the exact TradeLoop pattern.
2. **The trader-domain candidate is given one honest, bounded, zero-capital test by the founder's OWN hand this weekend** (see §4). Result: either the founding-level personal proof that existing replay tools adequately answer the edge question (then the trading domain is closed by the founder's own data, permanently), or a documented, structural gap (it enters the evidence file as a watch item — not a company bet).
3. **"We never beg people to buy" is re-stated as its verified equivalent:** every winner above had a distribution asset BEFORE monetizing (TradeZella: founder's 145k+ followers; FX Replay: 90% word-of-mouth + 20k weekly organic signups; TradingView: free tool virality + broker embedding). Distribution is built as an asset in the free tier era, not skipped. The TrustLayer plan follows this shape (embed at rails/PSPs so buyers' own operations distribute us).

## 4. Founder weekend self-experiment spec (zero capital, no product build)

Using the founder's REAL strategy (approach-2: value-area/heatmap retest entries), his own tooling:
1. Define setup, entry, invalidation, exit, risk rules in writing BEFORE picking examples.
2. Run 30 historical examples (TradingView bar replay or FX Replay free tier) — but hide the outcome first, record the call, then reveal (the sealed-test discipline, adapted).
3. Compute the results **including spread, slippage and commission estimates** — expectancy net of costs, with a simple count-based confidence statement (e.g., wins/30 with per-trade $ scale).
4. Same exercise for a trading friend's strategy too (1 more trader) — two data points, not one.
5. Write down what举行 remained impossible to answer reliably (e.g., selection bias from remembered setups, subjective criterion drift, cost uncertainty, no way to blind-pick examples).
6. One page verdict: **"Can existing tools already answer 'does my setup have an edge after costs?' without building anything?"** — YES: trading domain closed by own hand. NO with a structural gap: logged for later, not for building.

Total cost: one weekend. It either complements or supersedes the standalone Sealed Test concept — and is the only remaining legitimate use of the parked TradeLoop methodology.

## 5. Load this lesson forward (ratchet)

The spectacle demand itself is now a **validated user-need statement**: "they find us, they need us, we don't beg." Converted into design rules for whatever the gates produce: embed at rails; asset-side compounding; free evidence-tier before paid tier; judge-neutrality; standard first. Any future pitch must state which of the five mechanics it satisfies — or it gets rejected the way TradeLoop's was.
